# Derivation record

## 1. Parametric curve

Set `q=exp(i phi)` and rewrite all trigonometric functions as Laurent
polynomials in `q`.  This turns the curve coordinates into exact Laurent
expressions over `Q(k,i)`.

## 2. Implicit surfaces by coefficient matching

For the cubic relation use the sparse ansatz generated by

```text
k*x^3, k*x*y^2, x^2*z, y^2*z, z, k^2*z, k^4*z.
```

After substituting the Laurent parametrization, clear Laurent denominators and
set every coefficient of `q` and `k` equal to zero.  The linear nullspace is
one-dimensional.  With a convenient scale it yields `H1` in `README.md`.

For the quartic relation use

```text
x^4, x^2*y^2, y^4, x^2*y, y^3,
x^2, y^2, k^2*x^2, k^2*y^2, z^2, k^2*z^2.
```

The same Laurent coefficient matching again gives a one-dimensional
nullspace and hence `H2`.

This is ordinary linear algebra.  No elimination engine or Groebner basis is
needed.

## 3. Tangent scale

For a transverse intersection of `H1=H2=0`,

```text
V = grad(H1) x grad(H2)
```

is tangent to the intersection curve.  Direct substitution gives

```text
V(r(phi)) = Lambda(phi,k) r_phi(phi).
```

Using `Q=exp(3 i phi)`, the scale factors as

```text
Lambda = 2 A3(Q) B3(Q) / Q^2,

A3(Q) = Q^2-k(1+k^2)Q+(2k^2-1),
B3(Q) = (2k^2-1)Q^2-k(1+k^2)Q+1.
```

Therefore the time integral is a rational contour integral in `Q`.

## 4. Residues

For `0<k<1`, the two poles inside the unit circle are selected explicitly in
`src/verify_geometry.py`.  Their exact residue sum simplifies to

```text
T(k)/pi = (1+k^2)/(k(1-k^2)^3 sqrt(k^2+4)).
```

No fitting or guessed recurrence is used.

## 5. Algebraic equation and ODE

Squaring the closed form and clearing denominators gives

```text
k^2(1-k^2)^6(k^2+4)Y^2=(1+k^2)^2,
Y=T/pi.
```

Logarithmic differentiation gives the minimal first-order linear ODE.  The
included second-order operator is verified directly and factored as a
first-order left factor composed with the minimal operator.

## 6. Integer series

After `x=k^2/16` and removal of the pole,

```text
A(x)=(1+16x)/(1-16x)^3/sqrt(1+4x).
```

The rational factor has coefficients `(n+1)^2 16^n`, while
`1/sqrt(1+4x)` has coefficients `(-1)^n binom(2n,n)`.  Their convolution is
therefore integral term by term.
