PROOF OF RAMANUJAN'S E4 ~ K^4 IDENTITY
Triangle-Rectangle certificate, page 2
Plain-text working draft, version 2

STATUS

This is an editable proof-flow document, not final certificate prose.

The proof is elementary in structure:

  plane curve
    -> even quartic
    -> signature-4 period
    -> Legendre period
    -> modular period
    -> Ramanujan's E4-K^4 identity.

The curve changes are explicit algebraic or birational transforms.
The parameter changes are checked by exact ODE pullbacks. The final
page should show this chain without introducing unnecessary notation.


0. TARGET

Prove

  E4(tau) = (2 K(k) / pi)^4 * (1 - k^2 k'^2),

where k'^2 = 1 - k^2 and tau is the period ratio of the Legendre curve.

This is the identity recorded by Ramanujan. Historical notation and
additional references can remain in the embedded payload.


1. THE NORMALIZED RECTANGLE-TRIANGLE FAMILY

Start directly with

  2 H_mu(p,q)
    = p^2 + q^2
      + (q^3 - 3 p^2 q)
      + mu * (q^2 - 3 p^2)^2.

The cubic term is the triangle contribution. The quartic term is the
rectangle contribution. The single parameter mu measures their relative
strength after normalization.


2. WHY mu = 1/4?

Direct substitution gives

  H_mu(p,-2-q) - H_mu(p,q)
    = -(4 mu - 1)
      * (q + 1)
      * (3 p^2 - q^2 - 2 q - 2).

Therefore mu = 1/4 is the unique member invariant under

  q -> -2 - q.

The shift

  x = q + 1

centers this reflection at x = 0.

The fixed point

  (p,q) = (0,-1)

is the hyperbolic saddle between the two wells. It lies on the critical
energy

  alpha = 2H = 1/4.


3. PLANE CURVE TO EVEN QUARTIC

Set mu = 1/4 and write

  2H = alpha.

Use

  u = q - sqrt(3) p,
  v = q + sqrt(3) p,
  x = u + 1,

together with

  A(u) = u^2/4 + u/2 + 1/3,
  B(u) = u^2/2 + u/3,
  y = 2 A(u) v + B(u).

Then the energy curve becomes

  12 y^2
    = -x^4 + (2 + 12 alpha) x^2 + 4 alpha - 1,

and the period differential becomes

  omega = dx / (sqrt(3) y).

The inverse map is

  u = x - 1,
  v = (y - B(u)) / (2 A(u)),
  q = (u + v) / 2,
  p = (v - u) / (2 sqrt(3)).

Thus the original energy curve and the even quartic have the same period.


4. SIGNATURE-4 PERIOD

Set

  A0 = 1 + 6 alpha,
  d  = sqrt(1 - 4 alpha),

and define

  z4(alpha)
    = 1 - d^2/A0^2
    = 4 alpha (4 + 9 alpha) / (1 + 6 alpha)^2.

The even quartic can be scaled to a reciprocal Jacobi-type quartic.
The useful point is that its period parameter z4 is rational in alpha.

The normalized period is

  T1(alpha)
    = (1 + 6 alpha)^(-1/2)
      * F(1/4,3/4;1;z4(alpha)).

Here F(a,b;c;z) is the Gauss hypergeometric function.

This formula is checked by one ODE pullback:

  1. take the Gauss equation for F(1/4,3/4;1;z);
  2. substitute z = z4(alpha);
  3. include the factor (1 + 6 alpha)^(-1/2);
  4. recover the page-1 Picard-Fuchs equation;
  5. use T1(0) = 1 to select the period branch.


5. EVEN QUARTIC TO LEGENDRE FORM

For 0 < alpha < 1/4, define

  R_plus  = 1 + 6 alpha + 2 sqrt(alpha (4 + 9 alpha)),
  R_minus = 1 + 6 alpha - 2 sqrt(alpha (4 + 9 alpha)),

  a = sqrt(R_plus),
  b = sqrt(R_minus),

  k = (a - b) / (a + b),
  m = k^2.

Then

  12 y^2 = (a^2 - x^2)(x^2 - b^2),

and

  m(alpha)
    = (1 + 6 alpha - sqrt(1 - 4 alpha))
      / (1 + 6 alpha + sqrt(1 - 4 alpha)).

Use the birational change

  U = k (x + b) / (x - b),

  V = sqrt(3) (U - k)^2 y
      / (b (a - b)).

It gives

  V^2 = U(U - 1)(U - m),

and

  omega = -2/(a + b) * dU/V.

Therefore

  F(1/2,1/2;1;m) = 2 K(k)/pi,

and

  T1(alpha)
    = 2/(a + b) * F(1/2,1/2;1;m)
    = 4 K(k) / (pi (a + b)).

The two period parameters satisfy

  z4 = 4m/(1 + m)^2,

or, on the branch m(0) = 0,

  m = (1 - sqrt(1 - z4))
      / (1 + sqrt(1 - z4)).

The corresponding quadratic pullback is

  F(1/4,3/4;1;4m/(1+m)^2)
    = sqrt(1+m) * F(1/2,1/2;1;m).

Together with

  (a + b)^2 (1 + m) = 4(1 + 6 alpha),

this proves that the signature-4 and Legendre formulas are the same
period.


6. WALK THE ENERGY PARAMETER TO THE MODULAR FORM

The whole parameter chain is

  alpha
    -> z4(alpha)
    -> m(alpha)
    -> X(alpha) = 1728/j.

For the Legendre parameter,

  X(m)
    = 27 m^2 (1 - m)^2
      / (4 (1 - m + m^2)^3).

Eliminating m gives

  X(z4)
    = 27 z4^2 (1 - z4)
      / (4 - 3 z4)^3.

Substituting z4(alpha) gives

  X(alpha)
    = -27 alpha^2 (4 alpha - 1) (9 alpha + 4)^2
      / (4 (1 + 9 alpha^2)^3).

The next ODE pullback is

  F(1/12,5/12;1;X(z4))
    = (1 - 3 z4/4)^(1/4)
      * F(1/4,3/4;1;z4),

with both sides equal to 1 at z4 = 0.

Since

  1 - 3 z4/4
    = (1 + 9 alpha^2)/(1 + 6 alpha)^2,

the same period has the three forms

  T1(alpha)
    = (1 + 6 alpha)^(-1/2)
      * F(1/4,3/4;1;z4(alpha))

    = 2/(a + b)
      * F(1/2,1/2;1;m(alpha))

    = (1 + 9 alpha^2)^(-1/4)
      * F(1/12,5/12;1;X(alpha)).

This is the central display for page 2.


7. FINAL ODE PULLBACK TO E4

As a function of

  X = 1728/j(tau),

the normalized function

  G(X) = E4(tau)^(1/4)

satisfies

  X(1-X) G''
    + (1 - 3X/2) G'
    - 5G/144
    = 0,

with

  G(0) = 1.

Therefore

  F(1/12,5/12;1;1728/j(tau))
    = E4(tau)^(1/4).

This is the final ODE identification. It does not assume the target
E4-K^4 identity.


8. CONCLUSION

From the common period,

  E4(tau)^(1/4)
    = (1 + 9 alpha^2)^(1/4)
      * 4 K(k)/(pi (a + b)).

Also,

  (a + b)^4 (1 - m + m^2)
    = 16(1 + 9 alpha^2).

Raise the period identity to the fourth power:

  E4(tau)
    = (2 K(k)/pi)^4
      * (1 - m + m^2).

Since

  m = k^2,
  k'^2 = 1 - k^2,

we have

  1 - m + m^2
    = 1 - k^2 + k^4
    = 1 - k^2 k'^2.

Hence

  E4(tau)
    = (2 K(k)/pi)^4
      * (1 - k^2 k'^2).

This is Ramanujan's recorded E4-K^4 identity.


9. FINAL PAGE-2 LAYOUT

Block 1: Special Hamiltonian

  State H_mu and show that mu = 1/4 is the unique reflection-symmetric
  member. Mention that q = -1 is the saddle between the wells.

Block 2: Birational quartic

  Give the map to

    12 y^2
      = -x^4 + (2 + 12 alpha)x^2 + 4 alpha - 1,

  and state the transformed differential.

Block 3: One period, three coordinates

  Display

    alpha -> z4 -> m -> X

  and the three equal hypergeometric period forms.

Block 4: Final ODE identification

  State the X-equation for E4^(1/4), the normalization at X = 0, and
  conclude that it equals F(1/12,5/12;1;X).

Block 5: Ramanujan identity

  Use the one algebraic prefactor identity and take fourth powers.

Detailed branch choices, symbolic residuals, historical notation, and
expanded ODE pullback checks belong in the embedded payload.
