PROOF OF RAMANUJAN'S E4 ~ K^4 IDENTITY
Triangle-Rectangle certificate, page 2
Plain-text working draft

STATUS

This is an editable proof-flow document, not final certificate prose.
The goal is to make every change of curve, period, and parameter explicit before
we compress the argument back onto one certificate page.


0. WHAT PAGE 2 SHOULD PROVE

The endpoint is the identity

  E4(tau) = (2 K(k) / pi)^4 * (1 - k^2 k'^2),

where

  k'^2 = 1 - k^2,
  tau = i K(k') / K(k).

Ramanujan uses the nome

  q_R = exp(-pi K(k') / K(k)) = exp(pi i tau).

The standard Eisenstein-series nome is

  Q = q_R^2 = exp(2 pi i tau),

so

  E4(tau) = 1 + 240 * sum_{n >= 1} n^3 Q^n / (1 - Q^n).

The page must not assume the E4-K^4 identity.  It should derive it from the
period of the selected plane curve, together with one independently proved
modular-uniformization lemma.


1. THE GENERAL RECTANGLE-TRIANGLE FAMILY

Start with the two-coefficient harmonic family

  2 H_{c1,c2}(p,q)
    = p^2 + q^2
      + c1 * (q^3 - 3 p^2 q)
      + c2 * (q^2 - 3 p^2)^2.

The cubic harmonic is the triangle term.  The quartic harmonic is the
rectangle/square term.

When c1 is nonzero, scale coordinates and energy so that c1 = 1.  The remaining
dimensionless parameter is

  mu = c2 / c1^2.

The normalized one-parameter family is therefore

  2 H_mu(p,q)
    = p^2 + q^2
      + (q^3 - 3 p^2 q)
      + mu * (q^2 - 3 p^2)^2.

This family must be stated before specializing to mu = 1/4.  Otherwise the
reader cannot see what has been specialized or why.


2. WHY CHOOSE mu = 1/4?

For the normalized family, direct substitution gives

  H_mu(p,-2-q) - H_mu(p,q)
    = -(4 mu - 1) * (q + 1) * (3 p^2 - q^2 - 2 q - 2).

If the formula is written for 2 H_mu rather than H_mu, the right side is twice
this expression.

Therefore mu = 1/4 is the unique member invariant under

  q -> -2 - q.

The shift

  x = q + 1

centers the reflection at x = 0.  This is the geometric reason the normalized
elliptic model becomes an even quartic.  It is also why mu = 1/4 is much easier
than generic mu: the branch geometry descends through x -> -x.

Proof-flow point:

  The special value mu = 1/4 is selected by symmetry before any modular
  identity is invoked.


3. BIRATIONAL NORMALIZATION OF THE PLANE CURVE

For mu = 1/4, set

  u = q - sqrt(3) p,
  v = q + sqrt(3) p,
  x = u + 1.

Define

  A(u) = u^2/4 + u/2 + 1/3,
  B(u) = u^2/2 + u/3,
  y = 2 A(u) v + B(u).

Then the energy curve 2 H = alpha becomes

  12 y^2 = -x^4 + (2 + 12 alpha) x^2 + 4 alpha - 1,

and the period differential becomes

  omega = dx / (sqrt(3) y).

The inverse map is explicit:

  u = x - 1,
  v = (y - B(u)) / (2 A(u)),
  q = (u + v) / 2,
  p = (v - u) / (2 sqrt(3)).

Thus this step is birational over Q(sqrt(3), alpha).

Recommended language:

  "birational normalization to an even quartic"

Do not call every later rescaling rational.  Standard Jacobi and Legendre
normalizations require chosen square roots of branch-point expressions.

The quartic discriminant is

  Disc_x[-x^4 + (2 + 12 alpha) x^2 + 4 alpha - 1]
    = -4096 alpha^2 (4 alpha - 1) (9 alpha + 4)^2.

Therefore the finite singular fibers are

  alpha = 0,
  alpha = 1/4,
  alpha = -4/9.

The zero alpha = 2/3 in the page-1 self-adjoint operator is apparent.  It is
not a collision of elliptic branch points.


4. THE SIGNATURE-4 PERIOD: PARAMETERS [1/4, 3/4; 1]

Set

  A0 = 1 + 6 alpha,
  d  = sqrt(1 - 4 alpha).

Define the rational signature-4 coordinate

  z4(alpha)
    = 1 - d^2 / A0^2
    = 4 alpha (4 + 9 alpha) / (1 + 6 alpha)^2.

After scaling x by a fourth root of 1 - 4 alpha, the even quartic becomes a
Jacobi-type reciprocal quartic.  The important point for the certificate is
that z4 is rational in alpha.

The normalized real period should be written as

  T1(alpha)
    = (1 + 6 alpha)^(-1/2)
      * F(1/4, 3/4; 1; z4(alpha)),

where F(a,b;c;z) denotes the Gauss hypergeometric function.

This is the requested [1/4, 3/4; 1] period form.

A compact exact proof can proceed by differential equations:

  1. Start from the Gauss equation for F(1/4,3/4;1;z4).
  2. Pull it back through z4 = z4(alpha).
  3. Include the prefactor (1 + 6 alpha)^(-1/2).
  4. Verify that the result is the page-1 Picard-Fuchs operator.
  5. Use T1(0) = 1 to select the correct local solution.

This avoids hiding an algebraic fourth-root scaling under the phrase
"rational Jacobi transform."


5. FROM THE EVEN QUARTIC TO LEGENDRE FORM

For 0 < alpha < 1/4, define

  R_plus  = 1 + 6 alpha + 2 sqrt(alpha (4 + 9 alpha)),
  R_minus = 1 + 6 alpha - 2 sqrt(alpha (4 + 9 alpha)),
  a = sqrt(R_plus),
  b = sqrt(R_minus),
  k = (a - b) / (a + b),
  m = k^2.

Then

  12 y^2 = (a^2 - x^2) (x^2 - b^2),

and

  m(alpha)
    = (1 + 6 alpha - sqrt(1 - 4 alpha))
      / (1 + 6 alpha + sqrt(1 - 4 alpha)).

The signature-4 and Legendre parameters satisfy

  z4 = 4 m / (1 + m)^2,

and, on the branch with m(0) = 0,

  m = (1 - sqrt(1 - z4)) / (1 + sqrt(1 - z4)).

Use the birational change

  U = k (x + b) / (x - b),

  V = sqrt(3) (U - k)^2 y / (b (a - b)).

This gives the Legendre curve

  V^2 = U (U - 1) (U - m),

and transforms the differential to

  omega = -2/(a + b) * dU/V.

Therefore

  F(1/2, 1/2; 1; m) = 2 K(k) / pi,

and

  T1(alpha)
    = 2/(a + b) * F(1/2,1/2;1;m)
    = 4 K(k) / (pi (a + b)).

The quadratic hypergeometric transformation is

  F(1/4,3/4;1; 4m/(1+m)^2)
    = sqrt(1 + m) * F(1/2,1/2;1;m).

Together with

  (a + b)^2 (1 + m) = 4 (1 + 6 alpha),

this proves that the signature-4 and Legendre formulas are the same period.


6. WALK THE ENERGY PARAMETER THROUGH THE WHOLE CHAIN

The page should display the parameter chain explicitly:

  alpha
    -> z4(alpha) = 4 alpha (4 + 9 alpha) / (1 + 6 alpha)^2
    -> m(alpha)  = (1 - sqrt(1 - z4)) / (1 + sqrt(1 - z4))
    -> X = 1728/j.

For the Legendre parameter m,

  X(m)
    = 27 m^2 (1 - m)^2
      / (4 (1 - m + m^2)^3).

Eliminating m gives the rational cubic map

  X(z4)
    = 27 z4^2 (1 - z4) / (4 - 3 z4)^3.

Substituting z4(alpha) gives

  X(alpha) = chi(alpha)
    = -27 alpha^2 (4 alpha - 1) (9 alpha + 4)^2
      / (4 (1 + 9 alpha^2)^3).

The second hypergeometric transformation is

  F(1/12,5/12;1; X(z4))
    = (1 - 3 z4/4)^(1/4) * F(1/4,3/4;1;z4),

with both sides normalized to 1 at z4 = 0.

Since

  1 - 3 z4/4
    = (1 + 9 alpha^2) / (1 + 6 alpha)^2,

all three period forms become one equivalence:

  T1(alpha)
    = (1 + 6 alpha)^(-1/2)
      * F(1/4,3/4;1;z4(alpha))

    = 2/(a + b)
      * F(1/2,1/2;1;m(alpha))

    = (1 + 9 alpha^2)^(-1/4)
      * F(1/12,5/12;1;X(alpha)).

This should be the central display on page 2.


7. THE MODULAR LEMMA AND THE NON-CIRCULAR ENDPOINT

The remaining modular lemma is

  F(1/12,5/12;1; 1728/j(tau)) = E4(tau)^(1/4),

with the branch normalized to 1 at the cusp tau = i infinity.

To avoid circular reasoning, prove this independently by showing that, as a
function of X = 1728/j, the function G(X) = E4(tau)^(1/4) satisfies

  X(1 - X) G'' + (1 - 3X/2) G' - 5G/144 = 0,

and has the local normalization

  G(0) = 1.

This can be derived from the Ramanujan differential system for E2, E4, and E6,
together with the definition of j.  It can also be checked by an exact
modular-form calculation plus uniqueness of the normalized local solution.

It must not be derived by first assuming the target E4-K^4 formula.

Now use the algebraic identity

  (a + b)^4 (1 - m + m^2) = 16 (1 + 9 alpha^2).

The period equivalence and modular lemma give

  E4(tau)^(1/4)
    = (1 + 9 alpha^2)^(1/4) T1(alpha)
    = (1 + 9 alpha^2)^(1/4) * 4 K(k)/(pi (a + b)).

Raise both sides to the fourth power:

  E4(tau)
    = (2 K(k)/pi)^4 * (1 - m + m^2).

Since m = k^2 and k'^2 = 1 - k^2,

  1 - m + m^2
    = 1 - k^2 + k^4
    = 1 - k^2 k'^2.

Therefore

  E4(tau) = (2 K(k)/pi)^4 * (1 - k^2 k'^2).


8. IS THIS RAMANUJAN'S ASSUMED IDENTITY?

The final equality is the identity Ramanujan records in section 9 of
"Modular equations and approximations to pi."

However, the entire curve-period chain is not itself Ramanujan's identity.
It becomes a proof only after the modular lemma

  F(1/12,5/12;1;1728/j) = E4^(1/4)

has been proved independently.

If that lemma is justified by first assuming the E4-K^4 formula, the argument
is circular.

Recommended summary sentence:

  The plane curve forces one period through the signature-4, Legendre, and
  modular hypergeometric coordinates.  An independent modular differential-
  equation calculation identifies the final normalized solution with
  E4^(1/4).  The resulting fourth-power equality is Ramanujan's recorded
  E4-K^4 identity.


9. PROPOSED FINAL PAGE-2 FLOW

Stage 1. Family and special value

  Introduce (c1,c2), reduce to mu = c2/c1^2, and prove that mu = 1/4 is the
  unique reflection-symmetric value.

Stage 2. Plane curve to even quartic

  Give the birational map, transformed differential, and quartic discriminant.

Stage 3. Hypergeometric ladder

  Display alpha -> z4 -> m -> X, together with the [1/4,3/4], [1/2,1/2],
  and [1/12,5/12] period forms.

Stage 4. Modular lemma

  State exactly how E4^(1/4) is identified without assuming the target identity.

Stage 5. Conclusion

  Use the algebraic prefactor identity to obtain Ramanujan's formula.

The branch-point details and the apparent-singularity remark can remain, but
they should support this chain rather than interrupt it.


10. OPEN DECISIONS BEFORE TYPESETTING PAGE 2

1. Show the full two-parameter family (c1,c2), or describe it in one sentence
   before writing mu = c2/c1^2?

2. Use "Jacobi form" for the even reciprocal quartic, or display a separately
   scaled standard Jacobi quartic?  The first choice is cleaner and avoids
   pretending the fourth-root scaling is rational over Q(alpha).

3. Prove the [1/4,3/4] formula by direct integral substitution or by exact
   pullback of the Gauss equation?  The ODE pullback is shorter and easier to
   audit.

4. How much of the modular differential-equation proof fits on the page?  At
   minimum, state the differential equation and cusp normalization.  Put the
   full symbolic derivation in the embedded payload.

5. Keep the plane coordinate q, Ramanujan's nome q_R, and the standard modular
   nome Q visibly distinct.
